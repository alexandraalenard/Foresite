[ The problem ]

Most local websites were built for people. None of them were built for AI.

People are starting to ask ChatGPT, Google's AI answers and Perplexity for recommendations before they ever search the old way.

37%

of consumers now start their search with an AI tool instead of Google.

Source: Eight Oh Two 2026 AI & Search Behavior Study (US survey)

Most of the hard numbers so far come out of the US, where AI search took off first — but the same shift is already underway here in Australia, and it's moving quickly. The businesses that get ahead of it are the ones that won't have to scramble later.

Here's the real difference. Search the old way, and Google hands you ten options — you click through and compare them yourself. Ask an AI tool the same question, and it doesn't hand you a list. It reads what it can find, picks one or two it trusts most, and just tells you: "this is the one." If your business isn't the one it picks, you don't show up on page two — you often don't show up at all.

If your site is slow, hard to read, or missing basic structure, it's not just people who skip past it — the AI tools scanning the web to answer questions do too. A nicer-looking site is only half the fix. The other half is making sure the site is actually built to be read, understood and recommended.

Foresite does both at once: a properly modern rebuild, plus the groundwork that future-proofs your site — so AI agents can actually read, trust and recommend your business, not just people. All explained to you in plain English, not jargon.

[ How we got here ]

Every leap in technology moved the finish line for how people decide.

Every jump in technology changed how people research and buy — here's both sides of that story, side by side.

1994–95

Amazon & eBay — trust is still the barrier

Online shopping becomes possible for the first time, but most people won't type a credit card number into a website yet. Buying research still happens offline — word of mouth, the phone book, driving past a shop.

1998

Google's PageRank — the tab-comparison habit begins

Search becomes ten ranked blue links instead of a directory. A new buying habit is born and stays dominant for the next 15+ years: open several tabs, compare them side by side, decide yourself.

2007

The iPhone — research goes wherever you go

Search moves into people's pockets. Buying decisions stop happening only at a desk — "near me" searches let people compare options mid-errand, standing in a car park.

2011–16

Siri & Alexa — one answer starts to feel normal

Voice assistants get people used to asking out loud and accepting a single spoken answer for simple questions — the first real crack in "always compare a list."

Nov 2022

ChatGPT — typing keywords becomes talking

Tens of millions of people start describing what they actually need in plain sentences and getting a direct answer back — instead of guessing the right search keywords.

May 2024

Google's AI Overviews — research starts skipping the click

An AI-written summary is placed above Google's results list. A 2025 Pew Research study (US) found people click through to an actual website just 8% of the time when an AI summary is shown, versus 15% without one — real research now often happens without ever visiting a business's site.

Now

AI-first search — the decision is made before you know it

37% of consumers now start their search with an AI tool instead of Google. In B2B buying, research firm 6sense found the typical decision is already about 70% complete before a business is even contacted. Increasingly, you're not being compared — you're being confirmed, or skipped.

What's next

Agentic AI — comparison disappears entirely

AI assistants are starting to move from recommending businesses to acting on people's behalf — comparing, booking and buying automatically. The person may never actively "choose" at all. A business an AI agent can't read or trust won't just rank lower. It won't be part of the conversation.
